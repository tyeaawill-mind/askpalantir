What Is a Palantir AIP Bootcamp?

Not affiliated with Palantir Technologies Inc. This page is an independent explainer based on publicly available sources.

An AIP Bootcamp is Palantir's hands-on sales and onboarding format for its Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) — a short, intensive session where a prospective or existing customer builds a working AI application using their own real data, alongside Palantir's own engineers.

How it works

Bootcamps typically run one to five days and bring together business decision-makers, subject-matter experts, and IT staff from the customer's side with Forward Deployed Engineers from Palantir. One commonly cited structure breaks a five-day bootcamp into stages: initial preparation, then connecting data sources and building out the Ontology, followed by building the actual application and workflow, ending with a demonstration and decision point. The stated goal is to land a working prototype in a live environment within days, rather than the months a from-scratch software build would normally take.

Why Palantir uses this format

Bootcamps function as an early step in Palantir's customer-acquisition approach — sometimes described as the "Acquire" phase, referenced on our page about how Palantir makes money. Rather than a traditional sales pitch, Palantir uses the bootcamp to let a customer experience the platform directly, working with their own data and their own problems from day one. Palantir has described the sessions as largely free or low-cost to attend, positioning them as a way to compress a typical enterprise software sales cycle from months down to weeks.

Scale and growth

Reporting on Palantir's bootcamp program describes rapid expansion: over 400 bootcamps completed in a single year, spanning several hundred organizations across dozens of industries, with the pace of sessions continuing to climb into 2025 and 2026. Example use cases developed at bootcamps reportedly span dynamic scheduling (flight adjustments, train routing, healthcare provider schedules), commercial underwriting, sales and marketing workflows, and procurement cost analysis.

What attendees have said

Secondhand accounts shared publicly describe bootcamps as accessible to non-technical participants — one recurring theme is that AIP's Workshop tool can generate application interfaces automatically based on predefined actions, letting people without coding backgrounds build working tools directly. We'd treat individual attendee accounts as anecdotal rather than independently verified, consistent with how we handle secondhand sources throughout this site.

Sources consulted: Palantir's own blog (deploying-full-spectrum-ai-in-days), instinctools, PVM, gtmfoundry.vc, and a Baidu Baike summary of bootcamp mechanics. This page will be updated as the program evolves.
