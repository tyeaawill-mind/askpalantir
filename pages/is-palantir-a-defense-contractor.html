Is Palantir a Defense Contractor?

Not affiliated with Palantir Technologies Inc. This page is an independent explainer based on publicly available sources.

Yes — Palantir holds active contracts with defense, intelligence, and law enforcement agencies, and describes supporting Western militaries and security agencies as a central part of its mission. At the same time, defense work is only part of the company's business; a growing share of revenue now comes from civilian government agencies and commercial enterprise customers.

What "defense contractor" covers here

Palantir's government-facing platform, Gotham, has been used by agencies including the U.S. Army, U.S. Navy, and intelligence organizations for tasks like counterterrorism analysis, logistics, and operational readiness — the company has stated its software helps the U.S. Army track the readiness of more than a million military personnel across command structures. Palantir has also supplied software used in active conflicts; reporting has described its tools being used to support operations in Ukraine and by the Israel Defense Forces.

Beyond core defense: law enforcement and civilian agencies

Palantir's government customer base extends past traditional defense into domestic and civilian use — including work with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), reported use by U.S. police departments for predictive-policing programs, and civilian agencies such as public health bodies. Some of these law-enforcement deployments have been controversial: cities including New Orleans and Los Angeles reportedly discontinued predictive-policing programs built on Palantir's analytics following public criticism.

International defense relationships

Palantir's defense-adjacent work isn't limited to the United States. In the UK, the company holds contracts with the Ministry of Defence and, more controversially, a £330 million, seven-year contract to run the NHS Federated Data Platform — a civilian healthcare data system, not a military one, but one that has drawn parliamentary scrutiny and public debate in the UK over a defense-linked company's role in health data.

The company's own framing

Palantir's leadership, including CEO Alex Karp, has been explicit that supporting Western governments and militaries is not incidental to the business but central to its stated mission — describing the company's alignment with national-security customers as a deliberate choice rather than one segment among many. This framing is itself part of why Palantir draws more public debate than most enterprise software vendors: the company doesn't present its defense and security work as separate from its identity, but as core to it.

The other side of the business

Despite this defense-forward identity, Palantir's commercial segment — serving companies like Airbus, BP, Coca-Cola, and various healthcare and financial-services firms — has grown substantially and now represents a significant and growing share of total revenue, alongside (not replacing) its government and defense work.
