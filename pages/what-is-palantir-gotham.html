What Is Palantir Gotham?

Not affiliated with Palantir Technologies Inc. This page is an independent explainer based on publicly available sources.

Gotham is Palantir's flagship platform for government, defense, and intelligence work — the product the company was originally built around, before it expanded into commercial markets with Foundry.

What it actually does

Gotham is designed to pull together massive, disconnected datasets — satellite imagery, intercepted communications, financial records, sensor feeds — and fuse them into a single, searchable picture. Rather than analysts working in isolation with siloed data, authorized users across an organization can search, access, and analyze a shared data foundation under a fine-grained security model that controls exactly who can see what, down to the level of individual data fields.

Core capabilities

Government contract documentation describes several standing features: geospatial mapping and network analysis for situational awareness, tools for investigative analysis across disparate data types, a "common operating picture" that lets military, law enforcement, and intelligence users coordinate around the same intelligence, and traceable lineage so every change to data in the system can be audited. Gotham also incorporates Foundry's underlying capabilities, giving it access to Foundry's broader library of analytical and operational applications.

Who uses it

Gotham's customer base is concentrated in defense, intelligence, and law enforcement — the U.S. military and intelligence community were among Palantir's earliest customers, and Gotham has since expanded to allied governments. See our separate page on Palantir's biggest customers for specific named examples, and our page on whether Palantir is a defense contractor for more on this side of the business.

How it relates to Palantir's other products

Gotham is one of four core Palantir products. Foundry is the commercial-facing counterpart built on similar underlying technology; AIP layers AI capabilities on top of both; Apollo is the infrastructure layer that deploys and updates all of them, including in disconnected or classified environments. See our main explainer on what Palantir does for how all four fit together.
