Palantir vs. Anduril: What's the Actual Difference?

Not affiliated with Palantir Technologies Inc. This page is an independent explainer based on publicly available sources. Nothing here is financial advice.

Palantir and Anduril are the two most-watched venture-backed defense technology companies in the U.S. as of 2026 — and increasingly, they're collaborators as much as competitors.

The core difference

Palantir's core strength is software: fusing and analyzing enormous, disparate datasets through Gotham and Foundry, then increasingly acting on that data through AIP. Anduril, founded by Palmer Luckey after leaving Meta, builds hardware-first autonomous systems — drones, sensors, and its Lattice AI platform, which integrates cameras, sensors, and other assets into a real-time, largely autonomous surveillance and tracking system. One industry comparison frames it simply: Palantir processes and makes sense of data; Anduril builds the physical, autonomous systems that collect it and act in the field.

Financial scale, as of 2026

The two companies sit at very different points in size and public-market status. Palantir is valued at roughly $312 billion and has raised about $2.7 billion since founding; Anduril is valued at roughly $14 billion, having raised a much larger $10.6 billion, and remains privately held as of this writing. Federal contract tracking puts Palantir's cumulative government awards at roughly $5.0 billion against Anduril's roughly $2.8 billion, with Palantir's total skewing more toward the Department of Defense, Treasury, and health agencies, and Anduril's split more evenly between the Pentagon and the Department of Homeland Security.

Increasingly, partners rather than pure rivals

In December 2024, Anduril and Palantir formed a consortium combining Palantir's data fusion and AI decision-support with Anduril's Lattice autonomy and edge-computing hardware — explicitly aimed at solving two separate problems together: large-scale data readiness and processing at the tactical edge in contested environments. That collaboration has continued to expand; both companies are reported to be co-developing core software for Golden Dome, a U.S. space-based missile-defense program whose budget has grown to roughly $185 billion, alongside other firms including Scale AI and SpaceX.

Where they still compete

Despite the partnership on specific programs, the two companies pursue overlapping government relationships and are frequently named together as the leading edge of a broader shift in U.S. defense procurement — away from traditional, slow-moving contractors and toward venture-backed firms that iterate quickly. Some coverage frames this as a "dual-track" dynamic within the Pentagon: parallel efforts by different service branches and command structures that keep multiple vendors (including Palantir, Anduril, and others like Microsoft and Lockheed Martin) developing competing or complementary systems rather than granting either company sole-source dominance.
