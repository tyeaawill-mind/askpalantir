What Was Palantir's Role in DOGE?

Not affiliated with Palantir Technologies Inc. This is a contested, still-developing story — we've laid out what's documented and what remains disputed. Sources are cited throughout.

DOGE — the Department of Government Efficiency — was a Trump administration initiative established by executive order on January 20, 2025, created by renaming the existing U.S. Digital Service. Its stated goal was modernizing federal technology, increasing productivity, and cutting spending and regulation. Palantir's connection to DOGE is real and documented in several respects, but the more serious allegations against the company remain contested rather than proven.

What's documented

According to The New York Times, Palantir's expanded federal data work in 2025 was influenced in part by DOGE, alongside a March 2025 executive order from President Trump promoting inter-agency data sharing. Several DOGE personnel are reported to be former Palantir employees, and other former Palantir staff have been placed in senior government technology roles, including federal chief information officer. Separately, Wired reported Palantir participated in a DOGE-linked effort to help build a "mega API" intended to allow broader access to IRS records — connected to the same period during which Palantir's IRS contract value grew substantially, detailed further on our dedicated IRS contract page.

The more serious, contested claim

The strongest version of this story circulating publicly is that DOGE "handed over" Americans' most sensitive federal records to Palantir to build a privatized surveillance system. An independent fact-check of that claim found it not fully supported by available reporting: journalists have documented plans, contract awards, and Palantir's expanded federal role, but have also recorded Palantir's own denials that it is building a single searchable "mega-database," alongside limited public transparency about exactly how datasets would be linked, who could access them, and how long data would be retained. The fact-check's conclusion was that the allegation is a serious, credible claim reported by multiple outlets, but not conclusively proven with the evidence available.

What Palantir has said

As detailed further on our pages about the IRS contract and about whether Palantir spies on Americans, Palantir's consistent public position in response to congressional inquiries has been that it does not itself control the underlying government data — that the systems are operated by the federal agencies that own the data, with Palantir providing the software layer rather than independently holding or merging records.

DOGE's own status, for context

DOGE itself has had a short, unusual lifespan: established by executive order in January 2025 with an initial end date of July 4, 2026, and reported to no longer be operating as a centralized entity as of November 2025 — meaning the organization most closely tied to this controversy had already been wound down well before some of the reporting about it was published.

What's still unresolved

The exact scope of data Palantir's federal contracts touch, how that API effort and the IRS work specifically relate to DOGE's broader agenda, and whether any cross-agency data-linking has actually gone live as described remain open questions, as of this writing, pending further reporting and any results from ongoing congressional inquiries referenced on our other Palantir-and-government pages.

Sources consulted: The New York Times (via Nextgov/FCW), Wired, Wikipedia's DOGE entry, and an independent fact-check (factually.co) that reviewed the underlying claims across multiple outlets. Given how contested this topic is, we've distinguished documented facts from disputed allegations throughout.
