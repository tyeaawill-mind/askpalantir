How Hard Is It to Get a Job at Palantir?

Not affiliated with Palantir Technologies Inc. This page is an independent explainer based on publicly available sources.

By most public accounts, Palantir's hiring process is genuinely difficult and highly selective — some candidates and recruiters have called it one of the hardest interview processes in the tech industry — though difficulty varies noticeably by role, and Palantir itself has pushed back on the idea that the process is uniquely brutal compared to peer companies.

What the data shows

Aggregate Glassdoor data covering thousands of submitted interview reviews rates Palantir's overall interview difficulty at roughly 3.3 out of 5, with a little under half of candidates describing the experience as positive — figures that have varied slightly depending on which aggregation and time period is measured, but consistently land in a similar range across multiple independent trackers. Difficulty also varies substantially by role: Glassdoor's data specifically flags Backend Engineer and Recruiting Coordinator interviews as among the hardest, while Senior Systems Administrator and International Specialist roles were rated among the easiest.

For Forward Deployed (Software) Engineer roles specifically, independent interview-tracking sites report even lower pass-through rates, describing the process as "extremely selective" and noting that the large majority of candidates who reach onsite interviews do not receive an offer.

What makes the process distinctive, according to candidates

Recurring themes across candidate accounts and interview-prep guides:

  • A heavy emphasis on ambiguous, open-ended problem-solving rather than memorized algorithm questions — one interview-prep guide describes Palantir as testing "how you think when the problem is ambiguous," not whether a candidate can recite a known solution.
  • Multiple rounds (commonly cited around 5–6 for FDE roles) including behavioral, technical/coding, and roles-specific "decomposition" or "learning" interviews designed to see how candidates break down unfamiliar problems.
  • Deep drill-down into specific past projects during technical/behavioral rounds, with interviewers picking one or two items from a candidate's résumé and probing extensively rather than moving quickly through a checklist.
  • A relatively long timeline — average time from first contact to decision has been reported around 28–29 days, longer than several peer companies in similar data reported for comparison.

How Palantir itself frames the process

In a post on its own blog, Palantir addressed its reputation directly — noting that public accounts range from candidates calling it "the hardest interview in the tech industry" to others reporting an easier-than-average experience, and framing that inconsistency as a reason to be skeptical of any single secondhand account (including this page's summary of aggregated ratings, which necessarily blends many different individual experiences).

Bottom line

Palantir's hiring bar is real and role-dependent, and by the numbers it's harder than the average tech employer — but the industry's most extreme claims about it being the single hardest interview process anywhere reads more like folklore-by-repetition than a settled, measured fact, and the company's own difficulty varies significantly by team and role rather than being uniform across the whole organization.

Sources consulted: Glassdoor aggregate interview data, Exponent (tryexponent.com), The Interview Guys, Dataford, Taro (jointaro.com), Palantir's own blog, and discussion threads on Blind. Anecdotal forum sources (Blind, Reddit-adjacent threads) are treated as individual accounts rather than statistically representative data.
