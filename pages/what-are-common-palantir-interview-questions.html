What Are Common Palantir Interview Questions?

Not affiliated with Palantir Technologies Inc. This page is an independent explainer based on publicly available sources. See our separate page on how hard it is to get a job at Palantir for interview difficulty and process-length data.

Palantir's interview questions are less about memorized algorithms and more about how a candidate thinks through problems that don't have one obvious answer — a pattern that shows up consistently across the different question categories candidates report.

Behavioral and "decomposition" questions

A recurring format in Palantir's process is what candidates and interview-prep guides call a "decomposition" or "learning" round — the interviewer presents an unfamiliar, often ambiguous problem and evaluates how the candidate breaks it down, asks clarifying questions, and reasons toward a structure, rather than whether they land on a single correct answer. This format is used specifically to test comfort with ambiguity, which Palantir's own hiring materials and outside interview-prep sources both describe as central to how the company evaluates candidates, especially for Forward Deployed Engineer roles covered on our dedicated FDE page.

Deep dives into past projects

Multiple candidate accounts describe interviewers picking one or two specific items from a candidate's résumé and probing them in real depth — asking about specific technical decisions, trade-offs, and what the candidate would do differently — rather than moving quickly through a broad checklist of topics. This rewards candidates who can speak with genuine depth about a smaller number of projects over those with a long but shallow list of experience.

Technical and coding rounds

Standard technical interviews cover coding and system-design fundamentals similar to other large tech companies, though several accounts note Palantir's technical questions often come wrapped in a real-world, ambiguous scenario rather than presented as a clean, abstracted coding problem — consistent with the emphasis on ambiguity described above.

Role-specific variation

Difficulty and question style vary meaningfully by role and team, detailed further on our interview-difficulty page — Backend Engineer and Recruiting Coordinator interviews have been flagged in aggregate data as among the harder categories, while some administrative and specialist roles have been rated comparatively easier.

What to actually prepare

Given the consistent emphasis across sources, the most commonly recommended preparation isn't memorizing specific questions — Palantir's own blog has pushed back on the idea of a single standardized question bank — but practicing structured thinking out loud on ambiguous, open-ended prompts, and preparing to discuss a small number of past projects in real depth rather than rehearsing a long list.

Sources consulted: The Interview Guys, Taro (jointaro.com), Exponent (tryexponent.com), Glassdoor aggregate interview data, Palantir's own blog. Individual candidate accounts (including any referenced above) are treated as anecdotal rather than officially confirmed by Palantir.
